Privacy policy

Last updated 11 November 2026

Who we are

VoxCordis is a free, open-source desktop app made by Nucume. It runs on your own computer. There is no VoxCordis account and no VoxCordis server: we never receive your chats, memories, files, calendar or any other personal data, and the app has no analytics or telemetry.

What stays on your computer

Your companions, chats, memories, pictures, settings and logs are kept in folders on your computer. Chats and memories are encrypted on disk, with the key in your system keyring. Passwords, API keys and sign-in tokens are kept in your system keyring (or, on Windows, encrypted for your user account), never in the settings file.

What leaves your computer, and only when you choose it

  • A server-hosted brain. If you choose a server or provider (for example OpenRouter, OpenAI or Anthropic) instead of the built-in brain, the conversation you send it goes to that provider, under their privacy policy.
  • Web search and the companion's browser. If you turn on internet access, search requests go to the search service you pick, and pages the companion opens are fetched from their sites.
  • Downloads and updates. The app downloads models and its own updates from GitHub and Hugging Face, and checks GitHub for new versions.
  • Connected calendars. If you connect a calendar, the app talks directly to your calendar provider (Google, or your CalDAV server). See below.

Google Calendar

If you connect Google Calendar in Settings → Connections, you sign in on Google's own page in your browser and VoxCordis asks for two permissions:

  • calendar.events, to read your events when you ask your companion about your plans, and to add, move or delete events when you ask it to;
  • calendar.calendarlist.readonly, to list your calendars so you can choose which ones it reads and where new events go.

What is accessed. From your events: the title, start and end time, whether it is all-day, location, description, whether it repeats, and which calendar it is in. From your calendar list: each calendar's name, ID and whether you can edit it. The ID of your main calendar is your Google address, which Settings shows so you know which account is connected. Only events in the time span your companion asks for are read, from the calendars you ticked. Nothing else in your Google account is accessed, and no aggregated or anonymized data is made from it.

How the data is used. Only to answer you and do what you ask: events are shown to your companion when it looks at your calendar for you, and it adds, moves or deletes events when you ask. By default you see every change it wants to make, and nothing happens until you say yes. Google user data is used for nothing else.

Who it is shared with. No one but the AI brain you picked to talk to, and only so it can answer you. With the built-in brain, or a server on your own computer or home network, it never leaves your computer. Google Calendar only works with server-hosted brains whose providers don't train AI models on what they are sent: OpenAI and Anthropic (whose API terms exclude training on API data), and OpenRouter, which VoxCordis tells to use only providers that neither keep nor train on prompts. With any other server-hosted brain, VoxCordis doesn't give your companion your Google Calendar. Google user data is never sent to us, sold, transferred to data brokers or advertisers, or used for advertising or credit decisions.

How it is protected. All traffic to Google uses HTTPS. You sign in on Google's own page, with PKCE, so VoxCordis never sees your Google password. The sign-in token is kept in your system keyring (or, on Windows, encrypted for your user account). What your companion said about your events is kept in your chat history, which is encrypted on disk.

How long it is kept. Events are not copied or stored by VoxCordis: they are fetched when needed and kept in memory only while the app runs. The sign-in token is kept until you disconnect. Your chat history, which may mention your events, stays on your computer until you delete the messages, the chat or the companion.

AI and machine learning. Google user data is not used to develop, improve or train any AI or machine-learning model, by us or, as described above, by the providers VoxCordis allows.

VoxCordis's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The use of raw or derived user data received from Google Workspace APIs will adhere to the Google User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Disconnecting and deleting. Settings → Connections → Disconnect removes the token from your computer and tells Google to revoke it. You can also remove VoxCordis at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Other calendars

A CalDAV calendar (such as iCloud, Fastmail or Nextcloud) is reached with the user name and app password you enter, which are kept in your system keyring. A private calendar link is kept there too and is only ever downloaded from. The same rules as for Google apply: events are read and changed only for you, and go nowhere else.

Deleting your data

Disconnect any calendar in Settings → Connections. To remove everything, delete the companion folders and the app's data folder. There is no copy anywhere else for us to delete.

Children

VoxCordis is meant for adults and is not directed at children under 16.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. Questions are welcome on the Discord or in GitHub Issues; please report security problems privately, as the security policy describes.
